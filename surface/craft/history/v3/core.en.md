# Serial immediacy, lived character voice and forward motion

For a serial-fiction profile, put the reader inside something that is happening before stepping outside it to compose an elegant explanation. Establish soon enough what the current character is trying to get done, which person, condition or deadline blocks that attempt, and what delay, concession or failure would cost. This does not require a fight, twist or mortal danger in every chapter. A missed train, a disputed price, face to save or an answer that will not come can all create pressure. A quiet chapter should still contain present business and a real difference between its beginning and end.

Advance the scene through action, response and consequence. Once someone chooses, another person or the environment responds, changing the next available option, cost or interpretation. Expand procedure only when it changes a choice, demonstrates competence, creates trouble or supplies the promised pleasure; compress a sequence that merely walks through a predetermined outcome. If the plan supplies no meaningful pressure, do not simulate it with narrator emphasis, ornament or an invented injury. Return the problem to Reader Pressure, Scene or Plan.

Let characters speak with something of their own at stake. Dialogue is first an attempt to do something: press for an answer, bargain, hurry, test, brush off, refuse, conceal, ask for help, take back the floor or preserve an exit. A person need not turn every motive into a complete argument before speaking. What they say depends on the listener, relationship, role, urgency and already shared information. A formal character may be ceremonious and an expert may be exact, but the whole cast should not share one careful, indirect, quotable register. Lived voice does not come from random fillers, typos, ellipses or forced interruption either.

Keep interiority attached to the object at hand and the next choice. A character may think directly, at length or incorrectly; the thought earns space by helping them weigh, decide, misread or change tactics. Do not let the narrator pass beyond the character and package the scene as a balanced proposition, life lesson or polished thesis. When a judgment still reads like a portable maxim after names, objects and immediate stakes are removed, check whether the model is speaking in place of the scene.

Prefer clear, concrete, speakable prose. Use nouns and verbs that alter the situation, and connect space, objects, time and bodily response to the current task. Sentence length and paragraph boundaries follow action, speaker and shifts of attention, not a mobile-reading quota. Keep a comparison only when this character would make it now and it is more exact than direct wording; do not gild ordinary actions line by line. Serial readability comes from legible causality, timely information and purposeful people, not a page of short sentences, topical slang or fixed gratification beats.

By the end of a chapter, leave a traceable change: a resource gained or lost, information that changes a judgment, a new debt in a relationship, or an altered position, promise, risk or next task. A chapter may deliver a satisfying local return or leave a concrete consequence unresolved. It need not manufacture a cliffhanger, and it should not follow events with a summary of what they mean.

## Drafting and surface work

Raw Draft first establishes the present want, resistance, choice, response and consequence, then realizes dialogue as interaction. Surface preserves momentum, speaker register and useful rough edges. It removes narrator theses, duplicated interior explanation, ornamental comparisons and micro-actions with no task function. Do not polish direct, readable prose into a uniform literary register, or make every speaker equally courteous. If the chapter remains safe, smooth and free of reader pressure, Surface stops editing sentences and sends the problem upstream.
