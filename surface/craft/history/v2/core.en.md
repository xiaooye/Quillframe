# Narrative attention, distance and restraint

Start with what this moment lets the reader experience. The outline constrains events; it does not assign one paragraph to every item. Give changed understanding enough narrative time and compress connective work according to its value. Scene, summary, direct statement and omission are all available; quiet observation, craft, language and atmosphere may themselves be worth staying for.

Filter detail through the authorized viewpoint's interests, experience and diction. Interiority may be sustained thought, a brief judgment, an association or a plainly named feeling. Give it space when it changes a current interpretation, choice, mistake or way of speaking, or when thought itself is the promised experience. When it only explains what an action, exchange or object has already made clear, the prose can usually stop. Do not replace a character's specific judgment with generic clenched hands, bitten lips or heartbeats merely to reduce interiority.

Complete one layer of meaning once. When action, dialogue, consequence or an exact thought already makes the relationship, feeling or outcome legible, do not have the narrator name it again, translate it into theme or repeat it in abstract language. Conversely, do not call a genuine information gap subtlety; supply the observation, causality or judgment the reader needs.

Let sentences and paragraphs follow complete units of attention, action, exchange and thought, not a mobile-reading quota. A short sentence or one-line paragraph earns its boundary through timing, stress, voice or cognition, with omitted relations recoverable from context. Do not simulate speed through clusters of fragments, subtitle-like verdicts or mechanical long-short alternation. Keep a continuous inference or action continuous when splitting it makes it harder to read.

A comparison should be available to this viewpoint and add a precise shape, relation or sensation that direct wording does not. If it merely decorates an ordinary action, has no connection to the scene's lived experience, or joins a succession of changing images, prefer an exact noun and verb. Plain language is not a lower register by default.

An ending may rest on an action, line, object, judgment or unresolved question that still carries force. Once the concrete landing works, do not repeat the outcome abstractly, append a generic lesson or manufacture a universal hook. A complete reflection remains valid when reflection is genuinely the chapter's experience.

## Drafting and surface work

Raw Draft establishes scene causality, character response and natural syntax; it does not manufacture roughness through fragments. Surface preserves facts, character voice and useful texture. Its first moves are subtractive: remove redundant gloss and ornamental comparisons, recombine falsely separated units, and delete bodily annotations that add no information. Surface does not add thematic significance, aphorisms, emotional uplift or a summarizing coda. It may leave already accurate prose alone.

## Original miniature

“One key for now,” said the archivist.

Meng signed her name. Six keys lay on the desk; only the storeroom key came across to her.

She counted the other five before saying, “That's enough.”

The limit, interpretation and response already act on one another, so the narrator need not grade the amount of trust. Another scene may state that relation in thought. This example demonstrates stopping after meaning lands, not a ban on interiority or a key motif to copy.
