# Narrative attention and distance

Start with what this moment lets the reader experience. The outline constrains events; it does not assign one paragraph to every item. Give the moment of changed understanding enough narrative time, and summarize connective work when its exact performance offers little.

Choose among a scene, summary, direct explanation and omission. A scene makes a consequential exchange available to experience. A summary can carry elapsed time and a changed expectation. A direct sentence can orient the reader more cleanly than a staged demonstration. Omission works when the reader can bridge the gap without losing causality or a valued experience. Quiet observation, craft, language and atmosphere may themselves be that experience.

Within the supplied viewpoint, select details through this person's interests and interpretations. Interiority can be a sustained thought, a brief judgment, an association or an explicit feeling. Its length follows the mind and the scene, not a prescribed ratio. Preserve uncertainty; another person's unspoken motive is not made available by choosing close narration.

Shape paragraphs around units of attention, interaction and thought. Keep a continuous inference intact when it matters, then move the reader when the attention really moves. Carry a useful residue across transitions: an unanswered offer, an altered plan, a physical inconvenience or a changed way of addressing someone.

## Original miniature

The porter carried the boxes upstairs. By the third trip, Yun had stopped telling him which room was hers.

The summary compresses labor but retains a change in familiarity. A different chapter could dwell on carrying a single box if the effort, object or conversation is its central experience. The example is a choice of attention, not a sentence template.
