# Worldbuilding and wonder

Give the reader a point of contact with the unfamiliar: a familiar need, a comparison the viewpoint could make, an unexpected scale, or an ordinary action transformed by a new rule. The emotional response may be delight, unease, reverence or curiosity, not automatically danger.

Select a few related details that let the reader construct the place. A pattern across sound, use and human behavior can reveal more than a list of wonders. Let the viewpoint's history determine what is extraordinary and what is mundane.

Supply the explanation needed for the current experience or decision. A direct explanation, a demonstration, an overheard correction and a delayed answer are all available. Keep the relationship between the rule and its consequence understandable without requiring a full system manual.

Awe can deserve sustained narrative time even before it changes a practical choice. Where the story calls for action, let the new rule create usable possibilities and limits. Respect established knowledge: an evocative image is not permission to invent a new world fact.

## Original miniature

At dusk the stairway released its lowest step. It drifted to the next island, carrying a woman with a shopping basket. Ren had spent the afternoon admiring the architecture; only now did he understand that the city was about to move.

An ordinary journey makes the scale intelligible. A more contemplative chapter could remain with the moving city instead of immediately converting it into a threat.
