# Comedy and lightness

Establish the expectation the reader can recognize before bending it. The mismatch may concern status, competing priorities, a literal interpretation, or an extraordinary ability meeting an ordinary need. Let the result follow the story's actual rules.

Choose whose perspective makes the situation funny. People can pursue incompatible goals in earnest; they do not all need to perform as comedians. A dry narrator is another legitimate voice when the project has established it.

Give the beat the time it needs to land, then trust the reader. A reaction earns space through its particular knowledge or interest, not because every joke needs an audience response. Repetition can build recognition when the context, cost or owner of the joke changes.

Keep the chapter's emotional contract in view. Warmth, embarrassment, satire and dark humor ask the reader to laugh at different things. A comic detail can offer companionship or release without carrying a large plot consequence; it should still belong to this voice and moment.

## Original miniature

The oracle guaranteed three accurate predictions. The innkeeper asked whether tomorrow's rain counted as one or whether she could exchange it for the identity of whoever kept taking the towels.

The priorities clash without making either person incompetent. In another scene, letting the oracle answer the practical question seriously may be the funnier choice.
