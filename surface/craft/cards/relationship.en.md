# Relationship and emotional movement

Locate the immediate relational question. It may be whether help is welcome, whether a refusal will be believed, or whether an old form of closeness still applies. Let each participant bring a different expectation; symmetry is not required.

Choose the tactic through the listener and the relationship. A speaker may ask directly, make an offer easy to refuse, joke, teach, change the subject or state everything plainly. Subtext emerges from the gap between an interaction and what is at stake, not from compulsory evasiveness. A briefing, confession or apology may need complete speech.

Give the viewpoint character a particular interpretation to revise. Feeling can be carried by thought, word choice, attention, action or direct naming. Use the channel that contributes something new instead of adding a bodily annotation to every line.

Let emotional movement affect the next interaction. Someone can accept help but refuse its conditions, forgive without restoring trust, or enjoy companionship without a dramatic turn. The consequence need not be announced by the narrator.

## Original miniature

“I can collect your key,” said Mei.

Her brother read the message twice. She used to write “I'll collect it.” He sent the address, then added the time he would be home.

The distinction concerns permission and expectation. Its effect depends on established history; it cannot be imported into an unrelated relationship as proof of reconciliation.
