# Code of Conduct

Quillframe should be a place where people can disagree rigorously about software, writing systems, product design, and architecture without making participation hostile or unsafe.

## Expected behavior

Please:

- be respectful and specific when reviewing ideas or work;
- criticize code, designs, claims, and tradeoffs rather than the person proposing them;
- assume good intent while still asking for evidence and clear boundaries;
- make room for people with different backgrounds, experience levels, languages, and perspectives;
- keep private information, credentials, and unpublished project/manuscript material private;
- accept maintainers' scope, moderation, and repository-governance decisions even when you disagree with them.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or targeted abuse;
- discriminatory or demeaning remarks about protected or personal characteristics;
- sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention;
- doxxing or publishing another person's private information without permission;
- intentionally exposing API tokens, credentials, private manuscript data, or security-sensitive information;
- sustained personal attacks, trolling, or disruption that prevents useful technical discussion;
- retaliating against someone for making a good-faith conduct or security report.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other project-controlled collaboration spaces. It also applies when someone is clearly representing the project in another public collaboration context.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, close discussions, reject contributions, temporarily restrict participation, or block participants when necessary to protect the project community.

For an ordinary disagreement, use the relevant issue or pull request and keep the discussion technical. For harassment, doxxing, or other matters that should not be published, contact the repository owner privately using a contact method exposed on the owner's GitHub profile. Security-sensitive reports should follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Reports will be considered in context and handled as privately as practical. Deliberately false or bad-faith reports may themselves be treated as disruptive behavior.

## A practical standard

A useful test is simple: **could a technically serious newcomer read this exchange and still feel welcome to contribute?** If not, rewrite it before posting.
